Four rules that prevent most check-in failures: (1) never power up an uncoordinated transmitter with RF on — put it in its RF-off / standby / mute state first (see each card’s red RF OFF FIRST note), set the assigned frequency, then enable RF; (2) transmitter and receiver must be in the same band / block; (3) on Sony UWP-D & Wisycom, select the fine / unlocked group before an arbitrary frequency is reachable (Sony DWX also offers direct FREQ INPUT); (4) on digital systems an IR-sync (RX→TX) copies the frequency onto the other unit — re-sync after band or encryption changes.

  2. 2+/– to GP/CH; hold SET until GROUP flashes → pick GP 00 for free tuning (any freq on the 125 kHz raster); SET.

CANNOT BE COORDINATEDThese mics hop across the whole 2.4 GHz ISM band automatically (DJI: GFSK; Rode: “Series IV” digital; Hollyland: adaptive frequency hopping). No menu, sync, or app setting pins them to a frequency — on a coordination report they can only appear as a wideband 2.4 GHz occupant, never as an assigned channel.
WHAT THEY ARE All 2.4 GHz ISM, all auto-hop
  1. 1DJI Mic / Mic 2 / Mic Mini: 2.400–2.4835 GHz, GFSK, < 20 dBm EIRP (Mic 3 adds 5.1–5.8 GHz).
  2. 2Rode Wireless GO II / PRO / ME: “Series IV” 2.4 GHz digital, 128-bit encryption, auto-pairing.
  3. 3Hollyland Lark M2 / Max: 2.4 GHz adaptive frequency hopping, < 20 dBm EIRP.
WHAT TO TELL THE CREW Kindly, at the check-in table
  1. 1These can’t take an assigned frequency — that’s the design, not a setting you’re missing.
  2. 2They share 2.4 GHz with venue Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, ZaxNet, and comms — count them on the walk anyway; every one raises the noise floor.
  3. 3Power off units that aren’t actively shooting; if reliability matters, hand them a coordinated UHF pack instead.
SATURATIONSaturation is the endgame: each link needs clean hop-space. Cluster enough of them (plus Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, ZaxNet) in one small area and the band fills — all of them start dropping at once, and no settings menu can fix physics.
RF POWERFixed by regulation, not by menu: ISM-band devices run < 20 dBm EIRP and must tolerate each other’s interference (FCC Part 15). Range claims (100–400 m LOS) assume a quiet band — an event floor is not one.
HISTORY BONUSWhy they hop: frequency-hopping spread spectrum was co-invented in 1942 by actress Hedy Lamarr and composer George Antheil — US Patent 2,292,387, “Secret Communication System”. Every mic on this card is her great-grandchild.
WATCH OUT
  • Nothing on this card is field-tunable — if a crew insists their unit “has a channel setting,” it’s a pairing menu, not a frequency menu.
  • DJI Mic 3 in 5 GHz mode lands in the same U-NII bands as Teradek video — see the 5/6 GHz chart card.
  • These are fine tools in isolation; the problem is density.
